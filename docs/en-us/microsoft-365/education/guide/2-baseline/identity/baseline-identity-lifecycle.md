# Step 6: Set up your identity lifecycle

![](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/education/guide/images/pillars/icon-identity.png)

![](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/education/guide/images/sections/baselinesm.png)

Key to any identity system is the ability to manage the lifecycle of those identities within the organization. The identity lifecycle consists of on-boarding \(user provisioning\) and off-boarding users.

## Roles and responsibilities

- IT Admin
- Identity Admin

## Develop inactive and departed user cleanup strategy

Inactive and departed users are common throughout education tenants for various reasons. These users leave a security vulnerability and vector of attack for the organization and its users. They also consume storage and sometimes incur licensing costs. Admins should develop a strategy for identifying and deleting inactive users and departed users, and ensure this process is completed in an ongoing basis to ensure an adequate security and compliance posture to protect the tenant, its users, and their respective data. To accomplish this goal, education IT admins should minimally track students and educators who have left the organization each year and regularly run reports to identify any inactive users who might have been missed along the way, or whose accounts are no longer needed.

Note

Many education organizations have never cleaned up or deleted inactive users, graduating alumni, and students who were enrolled and eventually transferred to another school. Each of these inactive user accounts represents a vector for account and data compromise, in addition to inflating storage and licensing costs for the organization.
